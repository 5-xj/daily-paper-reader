Our algorithms sample a tree for every pair from an LP solution, but keep only its path between the two endpoints (for Ordered TSP, the pairs are consecutive given vertices). In particular, we extend their parity analysis from optimal solutions to fractional LP solutions, yielding an LP-relative guarantee for Multi-Path TSP.

1.2 Overview of the techniques 

Both algorithms are variants of the algorithm of Christofides and Serdyukov [10,16]. They choose a path between the endpoints of each pair (𝑠 𝑖 , 𝑡 𝑖 ), attach all remaining vertices to these paths by a forest, and then make the degrees of this forest even by adding further edges. Finally, the resulting multigraph can be shortcut into a feasible solution. The 𝑠 𝑖 –𝑡 𝑖 paths are sampled from an LP solution. Our algorithms use the same tree-sampling step as [3,8]. For each pair (𝑠 𝑖 , 𝑡 𝑖 ), an optimal LP solution gives a distribution over trees containing 𝑠 𝑖 and 𝑡 𝑖 . We independently sample one tree T𝑖 for each pair. Every vertex belongs to at least one sampled tree in expectation, and is missed by all trees with probability at most 1 /𝑒 . The expected cost of connecting the missed vertices is then at most 1 /𝑒 times the cost of a cheapest forest rooted at the terminal set 𝑇 ; in [8], this is the Bridge Lemma (Lemma 4), which goes back to Byrka et al. [9]. The algorithms of [3,8] include the sampled trees in the solution, at an expected cost of up to the LP value. For Ordered TSP, parity correction costs at most opt LP /2, as in the LP-relative analysis of Christofides’ algorithm [17,19]. Together with the cost of connecting the missed vertices, this gives an expected total cost of at most (1 + 1

+ 12 )opt LP . The construction of [8] duplicates the sampled-tree edges outside the prescribed endpoint paths, allowing those edges to be traversed out and back. These algorithms can all be derandomized by conditional expectations (see Appendix C for ours).
