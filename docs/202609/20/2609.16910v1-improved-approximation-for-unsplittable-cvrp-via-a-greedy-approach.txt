# Improved Approximation for Unsplittable CVRP via a Greedy Approach 

Daniel Ebert and Leonard Weismantel 

Research Institute for Discrete Mathematics, Bonn, Germany 

{ebert,weismantel}@dm.uni-bonn.de 

Abstract. We devise a polynomial-time 3.159 –approximation algorithm for the metric unsplittable Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem. We build on the Relative Greedy Algorithm suggested by Traub [6], which can be considered as a variant of the LP rounding algorithm of Friggstad, Mousavi, Rahgoshay, and Salavatipour [4]. Our main ingredient is the Average Greedy Algorithm, a new algorithm that controls both tour costs and the coverage of clients with high demand. This additional control enables a sharper averaging argument for the cost of subsequent greedy choices. Similarly to Zhao and Xiao [7], combining the Average Greedy with variants of tour partitioning and a matching algorithm yields the final approximation guarantee. 

## 1 Introduction 

The Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), introduced by Dantzig and Ramser [3] in 1959, is one of the fundamental problems in combinatorial opti-mization, with obvious applications in logistics. In the metric unsplittable variant considered in this paper, we are given a set V of clients, a depot s, edge costs 

c : ( V ∪ { s}) × (V ∪ { s}) → R≥0 that satisfy the triangle inequality and demands 

d : V → [0 , 1] . A feasible solution is a collection of tours, each starting and ending at the depot, such that every client is served by exactly one tour and the total demand served by each tour is at most one. The objective is to minimize the total cost of these tours. CVRP is NP-hard, generalizing the metric Traveling Salesman Problem and Bin Packing. Building on a TSP tour partitioning approach of Haimovich and Rinnooy Kan [5], Altinkemer and Gavish [1] obtained an (α + 2) -approximation algorithm, where α denotes the approximation ratio for TSP. Taking α = 1 .5

yields a 3.5-approximation algorithm. Only several decades afterward, Blauth, Traub, and Vygen [2] improved this by a small positive ϵ. Later, Friggstad, Mousavi, Rahgoshay, and Salavatipour [4] obtained a guarantee of α+ 11−δ +ln(2) 

for any δ ∈ (0 , 1/2) which gives a 3.194 -approximation for small enough δ. Their approach uses a modified tour splitting procedure that reduces the cost for clients with demand at most δ. Clients with demand at least δ can be partially served by sampling tours from a configuration LP. More recently, Zhao and Xiao [7] further improved this to below 3.176 by combining variants of the LP rounding approach with different tour partitioning procedures and a matching algorithm. arXiv:2609.16910v1 [cs.DS] 15 Sep 2026 2 Daniel Ebert and Leonard Weismantel 

In this paper, we improve the approximation ratio to below α + 1 .659 which gives 3.159 in the symmetric case. All of our approaches work for symmetric and asymmetric cost functions. Our improvement is based on an idea by Traub [6], which we sketch here: Instead of sampling from a configuration LP, one can greedily select tours, minimizing c(T )/σ (T ). Here, σ denotes a cost bound for the additional cost of serving the clients in T in the tour splitting procedure.
