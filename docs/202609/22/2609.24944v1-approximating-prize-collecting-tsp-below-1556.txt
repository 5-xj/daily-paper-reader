# Approximating Prize-Collecting TSP below 1.556 

Hong Li 

School of Mathematics and Statistics, Yunnan University 

honglimath@126.com 

Abstract 

The prize-collecting traveling salesperson problem is a variant of the metric traveling salesperson problem in which vertices may be left unvisited by paying their associated penalties. The objective is to minimize the length of the tour plus the total penalty of the unvisited vertices. Blauth, Klein, and Nägele gave the previously best-known LP-relative 1 .599-approximation. We show that a simpler version of their algorithm, obtained by omitting the splitting-off preprocessing before the tree decomposition, has an LP-relative approximation ratio of 1 .555761. The improvement comes from a stronger analysis of the parity-correction step.

The metric traveling salesperson problem (TSP) is defined on a complete undirected graph with nonnegative edge lengths satisfying the triangle inequality and asks for a shortest tour visiting all vertices. Prize-collecting variants of TSP were first studied by Balas [4]. For the formulation of PCTSP considered here, Bienstock, Goemans, Simchi-Levi, and Williamson [7] gave the first constant-factor approximation, an LP-relative 2.5-approximation based on threshold rounding. Goemans and Williamson [17] subsequently gave a 2-approximation through a primal-dual approach. The factor of 2 was first beaten by Archer, Bateni, Hajiaghayi, and Karloff [3], who obtained an approximation ratio of approximately 1 .979. Goemans [15] then combined randomized threshold rounding with the primal-dual algorithm to obtain a 1 .91457-approximation. Blauth and Nägele [8] later obtained an LP-relative 1 .774-approximation based on a refined decomposition of the LP solution into trees. Blauth, Klein, and Nägele (BKN) [9] obtained the previously best-known LP-relative 1 .599-approximation. The BKN algorithm starts with an optimal solution (𝑥 ∗, 𝑦 ∗) of (1). Several parts of the BKN algorithm build on earlier work. The splitting-off preprocessing before the tree decomposition uses the classical splitting-off technique [14, 24, 25]; the related parsimonious property for TSP LP relaxations [16] was used in early work on PCTSP [7]. The tree-and-matching construction follows the Christofides–Serdyukov approach for TSP [11, 27]; see [28] for its historical attribution. Variants of the same tree-and-matching framework also underlie the recent work that broke the long-standing 3 /2 barrier for TSP [19–22]. The tree decomposition used by BKN was first used in the PCTSP setting by Blauth and Nägele [8]. Its proof is closely related to branching-packing results of Bang-Jensen, Frank, and Jackson [5] and the polynomial-time construction of Post and Swamy [26]. Several variants and special cases of PCTSP have also been studied. The prize-collecting stroll problem (PCS), the path version of PCTSP with two prescribed endpoints, has been considered in several works [2, 3, 9, 23]. BKN [9] obtained an LP-relative 1 .6662-approximation for PCS. More recently, Li [23] showed that, for every fixed 𝜀 > 0, a polynomial-time 𝜌 -approximation for PCTSP yields a polynomial-time 

(𝜌 + 𝜀 )-approximation for PCS. Improvements in the approximation ratio for PCTSP transfer to PCS up to an arbitrarily small additive loss. Alimi, Mömke, and Ruderer [1] studied prize-collecting ordered TSP and prize-collecting multi-path TSP. # 5 Conclusion 

We proved an LP-relative 1 .555761-approximation for a simpler version of the BKN algorithm that omits the splitting-off preprocessing before the tree decomposition. BKN previously proved a guarantee of 1 .599 for their algorithm and reported computational evidence suggesting that an analysis following their proof could 17 not achieve a factor of 1 .59. Our analysis shows that the same algorithmic framework admits a substantially stronger guarantee through a different treatment of parity correction. The lower bound of approximately 1 .555623 established here applies only to the inequalities in (17), not to the algorithm itself. Obtaining a stronger guarantee for the same algorithm through an analysis beyond these inequalities is a direction for future work.
